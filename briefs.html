Pandæmonium · Anabaseios: The Twelfth Circle (Savage) · Athena

Athena Mechanic Briefs

Athena's mechanics in fight order, across both phases, each described twice: what the fight actually does, and the strategy the party uses to answer it.

Phase 1

Athena

1

Paradeigma I

Paradeigma · White Flame · Trinity of Souls

How the mechanic works

Paradeigma summons four yellow crystals, each spawning an untargetable Anthropos add. The adds all move to either the north or the south half of the arena, two on the west side and two on the east.

  • The adds fly out a pair at a time, one wall's pair about 5 seconds after the cast and the other's 5 seconds later, and fire White Flame in the same order, both adds on one wall together and then both on the other: a line AoE at each of the two players nearest to them, dealing heavy damage and Magic Vulnerability Up. That makes two sets of four lasers, which go off together with the first and the third cleave.
  • Alongside it, Trinity of Souls lights three of Athena's wings in turn, each telegraphing a half-room cleave on its own side, west or east. If a bottom wing glows first, she rotates 180 degrees between cleaves, so the second cleave lands on the half opposite its wing while the first and third land on theirs. The first use is always bottom to top.

How it is solved

Done without tank invulnerabilities, the party splits into its light parties: light party 1 (T1, H1, M1, R1) always baits the first wave and light party 2 (T2, H2, M2, R2) the second.

  1. Light party 1 goes to the adds' half and lines up down the middle of it to bait the first wave: ranged by the wall, then healer, then melee, and the tank next to Athena. Each add then picks the two players meant for it. Light party 2 stacks at max melee on the far side of Athena, where no add is near.
  2. After the first wave the light parties swap: light party 2 takes the same line-up and baits the second wave, and light party 1 stacks at max melee on the far side.
  3. Trinity of Souls always starts bottom to top here, so the safe side is opposite the bottom wing, the same side as the middle wing, and opposite the top wing. Everyone takes the line-up or the stack just on that side of the middle line. The pattern never makes the party switch sides twice. The wings stop glowing once the cleaves start, so read all three first.

Some groups skip the line-up: both tanks stand on the adds' half with their invulnerabilities and take every laser, while everyone else stays on the other half.

2

Paradeigma II

Paradeigma · Engravement of Souls · Ray of Light

How the mechanic works

Paradeigma summons six untargetable Anthropos adds, four from blue crystals and two from red. The blue adds take station near the four cardinals just off the wall, each pushed slightly to one side of its edge centre.

  • Engravement of Souls marks four players. One support and one DPS take Astralbright Soul; another support and DPS take Umbralbright Soul. When the debuff expires that player emits a small AoE inflicting Magic Vulnerability Up and leaves an aspected tower behind.
  • The four players with no Soul are each tethered to one blue add. Two tethers are light, two dark. Each add fires a line AoE down its tether whose damage falls off with distance, so the tether has to be stretched. Taking it applies Magic Vulnerability Up and the Tilt matching the tether colour.
  • A tower can only be soaked by a player holding the opposite aspect to the Soul that dropped it. Left unsoaked it casts Unmitigated Explosion and wipes the raid. Soaking deals minor damage and swaps the soaker's Tilt colour.
  • The two red-crystal adds appear at the north edge on either the first and third or the second and fourth column of the arena, then fire an untelegraphed line AoE (Ray of Light) straight down their column.

The tether lasers and the towers resolve at the same moment. The columns fire shortly after.

How it is solved

  1. Tethered players stretch straight across the arena, through the boss's hitbox, which both cuts the proximity damage and keeps the lasers off everyone else.
  2. Because every add is offset from its edge centre, the four tethered players end up one per quadrant. That turns the tower assignment into a quadrant sweep.
  3. Supports start at the north-east quadrant and sweep clockwise. DPS start at the north-west and sweep counter-clockwise. Stop at the first quadrant whose tethered player carries the opposite aspect to your own Soul, stand there at max melee, and drop your tower for them.
  4. The two sweeps run in opposite directions across the same two candidate quadrants, so a support and a DPS hunting the same colour always land on different players. The two supports hold opposite Souls, so they never compete either.
  5. When the lasers land, the tethered players gain their Tilt and immediately soak the tower beside them. Soul players can keep attacking at max melee until then.
  6. Everyone then reads the red adds to the north and moves into a column without one.

Superchain Theory I

Superchain Theory · Engravement of Souls · Impact lasers

How the mechanic works

Four blue clusters spawn on the intercardinals. Each is chained to one or more objects at varying chain lengths. Every object drifts toward its cluster and, on contact, makes that cluster fire an AoE determined by the object's shape. Shorter chains resolve first; two objects on equal-length chains resolve together.

ObjectWhat the cluster does
Green orbLarge point-blank centred on the cluster
Blue donutDonut AoE centred on the cluster
Orange spiked ballA narrow cone at every player, damage and Magic Vulnerability Up
Purple jackA narrow cone at either all DPS or all supports, split damage
  • Engravement of Souls splits the party by role group. One group takes a single debuff each: Astralbright, Umbralbright, Astralstrong and Umbralstrong Soul. The other group takes two each: Heavensflame Soul plus an Astral or Umbral Tilt, two of each Tilt.
  • The strong Souls call down a narrow split-damage line AoE on expiry, in their own aspect: Astral is the dark one and Umbral the light one, so Astralstrong fires a dark laser. It must be shared by four players, each of whom needs the opposite aspect, so the dark laser is shared with Umbralbright Soul and the two Umbral Tilts.
  • The bright Souls drop aspected towers as in Paradeigma II. Heavensflame Soul drops a medium AoE on the player when it expires.
  • Anyone who soaks a laser or tower ends up with a Tilt matching what they took; Tilts already held swap colour.

How it is solved

  1. Find the cluster with two short chains. It always carries one green orb or blue donut plus one orange spiked ball or purple jack, so it answers a distance call and a formation call at once: donut means in and green orb means out, orange means spread and purple means pair a DPS with a support. The spots are relative to Athena, as for the lasers: facing her from the cluster, the tanks stand in front, then melee, healers and ranged behind, the 1s on your right and the 2s on your left. To pair, each DPS stacks with the support beside it (M1 with T1, M2 with T2, R1 with H1, R2 with H2), midway between their two spots.
  2. Read your debuffs during that first resolution. Whichever role group holds Heavensflame is spreading at the end; the other group is on towers and lasers.
  3. Rotate 90 degrees, clockwise or counter-clockwise, to the cluster chained to a lone blue donut. Stay close to dodge the donut and split into two laser groups. Sides here are called from your own view, looking towards the boss.
Your sideWho stands there
LeftAstralstrong Soul · Umbralbright Soul · both Umbral Tilts
RightUmbralstrong Soul · Astralbright Soul · both Astral Tilts

The rule underneath: a laser can only be shared by the opposite family, so each strong Soul stands with the group named opposite to itself, because it is their own laser being shared. The sides follow the debuff icons: the figure on each Tilt leans to its side, Astral right and Umbral left, and each strong Soul's laser aims to its side, Astral left and Umbral right.

  1. Rotate again in the same direction to the cluster chained to both a green orb and a blue donut. The shorter chain lands first, making it either out then in, or in then out.
  2. Drift toward final positions while dodging. The bright Souls drop their towers on the two cardinals beside the last cluster, at max melee, keeping the same two sides as the laser split: facing the boss, Umbralbright on your left and Astralbright on your right.
  3. The two players who held the strong Souls soak the tower opposite their Tilt. Each strong Soul's own laser leaves it with that laser's Tilt, and each bright Soul drops a tower of its own aspect, so Astralbright drops the tower Umbralstrong soaks and Umbralbright the one Astralstrong soaks. Each pair already shared a laser group, so nobody crosses over: everyone stays on the side they took for the laser. The strong Souls wait in the middle between the two towers, just off it towards their own (further in by Athena when the green orb goes off last), until the bright Soul's small AoE has gone off, then step in.
  4. The four Heavensflame players spread well away from everyone else, in two pairs: the melee (the tanks, when supports hold it) run through Athena all the way to the far side, and the ranged (the healers) run back into the corner behind the last cluster, the 1s on the right and the 2s on the left as you face Athena from it. They never soak.

Paradeigma III

Paradeigma · Engravement of Souls · Unnatural Enchainment · White Flame

How the mechanic works

The arena is a two by four grid of rectangular tiles, which this mechanic finally makes use of. Paradeigma summons six adds: four from blue crystals, which take the east and west walls two per wall, and two from yellow crystals, which appear between tiles on opposite intercardinals.

  • Engravement of Souls marks the four supports: one Quartered Soul, one X-marked Soul, and two carrying the same bright Soul, either both Astralbright or both Umbralbright. Quartered leaves a plus-shaped AoE and X-marked an X-shaped one, and both are locked to true north regardless of which way the player faces.
  • All four DPS are tethered to the wall adds. Two tethers are light and two dark, and both adds on the same wall carry the same colour.
  • Unnatural Enchainment tethers four alternating tiles and destroys them at the end of the cast; anyone still standing on one dies. Four platforms survive, one per row on alternating columns, so the two middle platforms meet only at the arena centre.
  • A tower appears on each surviving platform and must be soaked by a tank or healer before the cast ends, dealing damage and Magic Vulnerability Up. Any tower left unsoaked wipes the raid.
  • Everyone receives Enchained Soul for the duration, which prevents jumping and so confines each player to a single tile.
  • The two yellow-crystal adds each fire a White Flame line AoE that needs two players to bait it.

Raid damage runs heavy throughout, so the set needs mitigation and healing. On the Soul afterwards restores the floor and lifts the chains.

How it is solved

Resolution is divided strictly by role, and because nobody can move between tiles, every position has to be right the first time.

  1. Quartered Soul takes the north-most platform and X-marked the south-most. The two bright Souls take the middle two, ordered west to east by a priority such as H1 > T1 > T2 > H2. Each support soaks the plain tower on their own platform. On a middle platform the tower sits in the outer half, right where the two DPS stretch their tethers, and their lasers run east–west across it. Soak it from the tower's side away from the arena's horizontal centre line, in the outer gap the two lasers leave, or a DPS laser clips the support. This is easy to overlook.
  2. Quartered drops its plus at the outer arena corner, where both arms run along the walls and miss the middle. X-marked drops its X at the cardinal wall point, where the arms run out to the corners instead. Both AoEs are delayed, so each support then steps out of their own arms and stays out while they bait a White Flame, aiming it away from the middle. An arm of the X runs straight through the southern add, so the X support baits from a step towards the outer wall. The + support has the opposite danger: the northern add's other line runs from it to the DPS baiting it, so they bait from behind the add, well clear of that line's edge, rather than tucked in right beside the add.
  3. DPS stretch their tether and cross to the middle platform on the far side from their wall, so both DPS from one wall share a tile, and the distance blunts the proximity damage. Whichever of the two adds lines up with the platform gives its player a straight tether: they stand at the platform's horizontal centre, level with the add, so the laser runs dead east–west. That is only just the minimum stretch the tether needs, so not a step closer to the add. The other has a crossed tether and stands at the middle of the platform's outer edge: top middle on the northern platform, bottom middle on the southern. Its laser angles across.
  4. Each bright Soul compares their own aspect against the tether colour of the two DPS on their tile. Matching colours cannot soak, so the tower goes on the arena-centre corner of the tile, where it spills onto the other middle platform. Opposite colours can, so the tower goes in the middle of their own tile, clear of the tether lasers.
  5. Both bright Souls share one aspect and the two walls carry different colours, so exactly one tower ends up at the centre and one stays on a platform, every time.
  6. The DPS pair whose colour is opposite the towers takes both: the crossed tether soaks the centre tower, the straight tether the one on their platform. The matching pair leaves the towers alone and baits the other White Flame.
  7. Supports who dropped a tower step aside, clear of every tower and of the plus and X AoEs.

The support priority is a convention, not a rule of the fight. H1 > T1 > T2 > H2 keeps each healer on their own side with the tanks adjusting around them, but other macro sets order the same four seats differently, so agree it with your party first.

Palladion

Ultima Blade · Palladion · White Flame · Theos's Ultima

How the mechanic works

Ultima Blade arrives without a cast bar, hits the raid hard, compresses the floor into a small octagon ringed by a lethal edge, and summons eight untargetable Anthropos adds. The adds on the cardinals carry a purple sigil and those on the intercardinals a yellow one. They fly off and return to the middle one per dash, in an order that changes between pulls.

  • Palladion makes Athena untargetable and drops her on a random corner of the octagon. That corner is relative north for the rest of the mechanic. Everyone takes a limit cut marker numbered 1 to 8.
  • She dashes to each number in sequence. The dash lays a line AoE along her path (Shockwave) that deals proximity damage. On arrival she fires a split-damage point-blank (Palladion) that has to be shared with a second player and inflicts Magic Vulnerability Up, and she leaves a blue puddle that kills anyone who touches it.
  • The adds return to the middle one at a time. A purple glyph means Clear Cut, a small 270 degree cone that can be ignored. A yellow glyph means White Flame, a laser at each of the two nearest players. The first four adds are two of each in a random order, and the last four return in the reverse of that order, so there are four sets of lasers: two during dashes 1 to 4 and two during dashes 5 to 8.
  • After the eighth dash Athena returns to the middle, destroys the centre of the floor, and casts Theos's Ultima, a raidwide split between everyone still alive. Deaths earlier in the mechanic can turn it into a wipe.

How it is solved

Numbers pair up as 1+3, 2+4, 5+7 and 6+8. Each pair shares both of its point-blanks and baits one set of lasers together, so the eight players reduce to four pairs doing the same job twice. Every position below is relative to Athena's corner, and anyone neither dashing nor standing in for the lasers stands against the wall.

PairStartsJob
1 + 3South cornerTakes the first dashes where they stand, then trades with 5/7 and steps straight in for the third laser set
2 + 4North-east corner, or anywhere in the northern tiles off dash 1's pathLets dash one land, steps into the corner Athena vacated for dash two, then trades with 6/8 and stands in for the fourth set once 1/3 have baited
5 + 7Close to the middle, west sideStands in for the first laser set, holds at the wall until dash 3 or 4, then takes dashes five and seven packed just clockwise of 1/3's puddles
6 + 8South-east wallWaits at the wall while 5/7 bait, steps in on the next dash, takes the second set, then takes dashes six and eight packed just clockwise of 2/4's puddles
  1. One pair always stands in close to the middle, on its own half (1/3/5/7 west, 2/4/6/8 east), in the bait order 5/7 → 6/8 → 1/3 → 2/4. Purple adds are ignored. When a yellow add appears, the pair standing in spreads apart, takes one laser each and goes straight back out to the wall. The next pair holds at the wall until then, so it can never be nearer the add than the baiters, and steps in on the following dash, straight into the lasers if that add is yellow too. Nobody else should drift inward.
  2. The reversal is what makes this safe: each pair's set always falls while it is free of dashes, 5/7 on dash 1 to 3, 6/8 on 2 to 4, 1/3 on 5 to 7 and 2/4 on 6 to 8. A pair still owed a dash steps in as soon as its dashes are done, which is always before its set can come.
  3. Only the first two dashes land in corners. Everyone Athena dashes to then moves clockwise to just outside their own puddle, as close to it as they can, and their second dash lands there. The puddles pack along the wall, 1-3-5-7 from the south corner and 2-4-6-8 from Athena's, which keeps the rest of the wall free for later and cuts the walking.
  4. Once a pair has taken both of its dashes it trades places with its bait pair, which steps just clockwise of the last puddle to receive the next two dashes. 1/3 step straight in off their dashes unless 6/8 are baiting on that very dash, in which case they wait at the wall first; 2/4 run clockwise along the wall until a puddle stops them and step in after 1/3 have baited. The two halves mirror each other, so the same movement repeats four times.
  5. After the last dash everyone edges north through the gaps between puddles and stacks for Theos's Ultima.

Limit cut numbers are handed out without regard to role, so unlike the rest of the phase this mechanic cannot be pre-assigned. The only thing worth agreeing beforehand is that relative north is read off Athena's corner, not off the arena.

Superchain Theory II

Superchain Theory IIA and IIB · Trinity of Souls · Parthenos · Unnatural Enchainment

How the mechanic works

The phase closes with two superchain sets. Both reuse the shape vocabulary from the first Superchain, but each runs another mechanic across the top of it, and the second set decides the fight.

IIA spawns three clusters at once, north, middle and south, all chained at the same time. The middle always carries a short green orb, a medium blue donut and a long green orb, so the middle of the room is out, then in, then out. North and south carry two shapes each, one short and one long, drawn from a fixed pool:

ShapeWhere it can be
Short green orbNorth or south, whichever does not hold the jack
Short purple jackNorth or south. This marks the opening cluster
Long green orbNorth or south
Long spiked shapeNorth or south, orange or purple. This marks the finish

Trinity of Souls runs alongside it. Three wings glow in sequence and each one marks the half of the room it will cleave. IIA always glows bottom to top, which makes Athena spin 180 degrees between cleaves, so the second cleave lands on the half opposite its wing while the first and third land on theirs. Apodialogos or Peridialogos follows immediately.

IIB spawns in three waves instead. First two clusters inside the middle, north and south, one chained to a blue donut and one to a green orb. Then east and west on medium chains, one a green orb and one a spiked shape that is orange or purple, while Athena casts Paradeigma and sends two red-crystal adds to the north. Last, north and south on long chains: one carries a lone green orb, the other a green orb and an orange spiked ball together. Parthenos lands between the first and second resolutions: an untelegraphed line aimed at the tank holding her, baited exactly north to south straight down her hitbox, covering roughly the middle third of the floor, which knocks back anyone caught in it. Unnatural Enchainment then destroys seven of the eight floor tiles during the last resolution.

How it is solved

IIA is three moves, and the whole read is available before the first one:

  1. Start at the cluster holding the short purple jack and pair up there, staying out of the middle for its short green orb, and on the half of the room the first cleave spares. The pairs line up from the arena centre outwards: T1 and M1 nearest the centre, then T2 and M2, H1 and R1, and H2 and R2 furthest out, so the order runs south to north on the north cluster and north to south on the south cluster.
  2. Run into the middle for the donut, dodging the second cleave.
  3. Finish at whichever cluster holds the long spiked shape, dodging the third cleave. If your opening cluster's long chain was green, the spiked shape must be on the far one, so run straight through; if it was orange or purple, turn around and come back. Orange spreads, purple pairs. Melee should stay clear of the middle's long green orb while resolving it.

IIB is slower and reads one wave at a time:

  1. Open inside the donut, on whichever of the two inner clusters carries it.
  2. Leave toward east or west, whichever holds the spiked shape. Either flank clears Parthenos, which the tank baits exactly north to south, but staying on the donut leaves you in its lane. Resolve the shape there: orange spreads to clock positions, purple pairs a DPS with a support, both taken relative to the boss.
  3. While standing in that resolution, find the columns without a red add in the north. Their columns fire next, the same four columns as in Paradeigma II.
  4. Finish at the cluster carrying both a green orb and an orange spiked ball, and take it out and spread: off the cluster for the point-blank, apart from each other for the cones.
  5. The surviving tile is always one of the four beside that final cluster, so you know which half of the floor to watch before the tethers appear. Step onto it straight out of the spread, while the cast is still running.

Athena then casts On the Soul twice, takes two more autos, and goes untargetable for a hard enrage. Her health has to be gone before that, so this set is both the last mechanic to survive and the last chance to push damage.

Pallas Athena

Gaiaochos I

Gaiaochos · Summon Darkness · Missing Link · Demi Parhelion · Geocentrism · Divine Excoriation

How the mechanic works

Pallas Athena opens with Ultima and a double Palladian Grasp on the main tank, which the tanks swap. Gaiaochos then deals heavy raid damage, compresses the floor into a small circle and gives everyone Ascended, which shrinks their model and slows movement. Sprint and similar speed effects cancel the slow.

  • Summon Darkness places three untargetable adds past the edge, each on a different cardinal or intercardinal line through the middle. Each charges a line AoE, Ultima Ray, down its line, so one line, with no add at either end, stays safe.
  • Everyone gets an overhead chain marker, and Missing Link then tethers each DPS to a random support. The chain has to be broken by distance before it resolves, or both players take heavy damage and Damage Down.
  • Demi Parhelion drops nine circular telegraphs in a three by three grid with gaps between them. Geocentrism shows how they will spread through the boss's tentacles. Tentacles wrapped north and south spread them into columns, leaving two narrow columns safe; east and west into rows, leaving two narrow rows safe; around the equator into a donut, leaving a narrow ring safe. The spread blasts linger for a few seconds.
  • At the same time every player is marked for Divine Excoriation, a small AoE with Magic Vulnerability Up, so the party has to be fully spread inside the safe ground.
  • Ultima follows, removing Ascended and restoring the floor.

How it is solved

  1. Stack in the middle for Gaiaochos and find the safe line: the one of the four with no add on it.
  2. As soon as Missing Link appears, run apart along that line. Supports take the end that lies between west and north-east, DPS the end between east and south-west. Every chain breaks at once, and both groups are clear of the other three lines. Then come back to the middle.
  3. Read the tentacles and spread into the safe ground by quadrant: T1 and M1 north-west, T2 and M2 north-east, H1 and R1 south-west, H2 and R2 south-east.
  4. For rows and columns, the support in each quadrant takes the inside of the safe strip and the DPS the outside. For the donut, the ring reads clockwise from north as T2, M2, H2, R2, R1, H1, M1, T1.
  5. Hold still until the blasts have faded. The Divine Excoriation AoEs are small, so the spots can be tightened if needed.

The quadrant seating is Icy Veins'. The tank buster before Gaiaochos is normally taken with a tank swap, saving invulnerabilities for the second Palladian Grasp at the end of Pangenesis.

The Classical Concepts I

The Classical Concepts · Shackled Together · Fusion Burst · Implode · Palladian Ray

How the mechanic works

The Classical Concepts hits the raid hard and puts a symbol over every head: a blue ✕, purple square, red circle or green triangle. Each symbol goes to two players, one with Alpha Target and one with Beta Target. A few seconds later the symbols vanish and each pair is joined by Shackled Together. If the two drift too far apart, both die instantly.

  • Twelve shapes appear in a grid of four columns and three rows: four blue icosahedrons (Concept of Water), four red pyramids (Concept of Fire) and four yellow cubes (Concept of Earth). Every column holds exactly one blue. Every blue touches at least one red and one yellow directly beside it, but never two of each.
  • Each red and each yellow then tethers to a blue next to it and fuses with it (Fusion Burst), wiping the raid. A player standing in the tether intercepts it instead and takes Aspect Effect, moderate damage plus Magic Vulnerability Up. Every layout has exactly one arrangement that blocks all eight tethers, and a failed attempt shows the correct tethers before the wipe.
  • All twelve shapes then Implode, a point-blank around each with only a brief telegraph. The shapes sit close enough that the only clear ground inside the grid is the gap between four of them.
  • Palladian Ray drives a tentacle into the west and the east of the floor. Each fires a lingering cone at its four nearest players, inflicting Magic Vulnerability Up. Taking a second cone, or staying in your own after it fires, kills.

How it is solved

Symbols pick the column and debuffs pick the shape, so every spot is fixed by two things a player can read in the first second. The common assignment is BPOG, west to east:

SymbolColumnTentacle
Blue ✕1, westmostWest
Purple square2West
Red circle3East
Green triangle4, eastmostEast
  1. Walk to your column together with your partner. Alpha stands between the column's blue and a red pyramid beside it, Beta between the blue and a yellow cube. The fight does not care which debuff blocks which colour. The split only makes the two of you pick different shapes.
  2. Often two shapes of your colour touch your blue. Take the one no other blue can use. Its twin is the only option left for a neighbouring column's blue, so it is already spoken for.
  3. When the shackles expire, step out of the Implode into a gap between four shapes on your tentacle's side, Alpha in the northern gap and Beta in the southern one. Waiting there already shows which way you will bait.
  4. Bait Palladian Ray from your own column, Alpha north of the tentacle and Beta south of it. The four cones fan out into an X with clear wedges between them, and none points across the middle at the other group. Once your cone has hit, step sideways out of it, then regroup for Ultima and Crush Helm.

Icy Veins also describes a 1234 reading of the symbols by stroke count: circle, ✕, triangle, square. It orders the columns differently from BPOG, so the party has to agree on one. The Alpha-north, Beta-south rule for the tentacles comes from the wiki. Some groups bait the cones by body language instead, which works but leaves room for two players to pick the same spot.

Caloric Theory I

Caloric Theory · Close Caloric · Pyrefaction · Atmosfaction · Ekpyrosis

How the mechanic works

Caloric Theory hits the raid hard. During the cast one random support and one random DPS carry an orange beacon, and when it ends one of the two is hit with a small split-damage AoE that needs at least two players in it. A blue grid appears on the floor to help with positioning, and every player takes a set of debuffs.

  • Close Caloric is on everyone. It gains a stack for roughly every 9 yalms walked, slightly more than the diagonal of one grid rectangle, and for every fire stack taken. A player who reaches five stacks, or dies while holding it, explodes and wipes the raid.
  • Four players take Pyrefaction (fire) and start at one stack. When it expires they release Pyre Pulse, a small AoE that must be shared with one player who does not have fire.
  • The other four take Atmosfaction (wind) and start at two stacks. The two beacon players are always among them. When it expires they release Dynamic Atmosphere, a circular AoE around them that spares only its owner and knocks back and kills anyone else caught in it.
  • The first fire stacks go off together. Two of the four fire players then receive Pyrefaction a second time and need two new partners from the players left with nothing, while the wind debuffs run out around them.
  • Ekpyrosis follows. Two proximity markers sit on one pair of opposite walls, and four exaflares start on the other pair in alternating lanes. After the proximity hit and the first exaflare, every player takes a large AoE with Magic Vulnerability Up, then the remaining exaflares march across the floor.

How it is solved

Icy Veins' method fixes everyone's opening spot from the beacons alone, then solves each move with a short rotation so nobody walks further than the grid allows. It uses waymarks A to D in a small diamond around the middle, with 1 and 2 further out to the west and east.

PlayerOpening spot
Beaconed supportD, on the grid line just inside the waymark
Beaconed DPSB, on the grid line just inside the waymark
Support counterpartA, the same job as the beaconed support in the other light party
DPS counterpartC, the same job as the beaconed DPS in the other light party
Everyone elseThe middle, between all four waymarks
  1. When the debuffs land, each of the four middle players walks out to a partner with the other element. Supports pick first, then DPS. Light party 1 checks D, C, B, A, counter-clockwise; light party 2 checks A, B, C, D, clockwise. Skip anyone already taken, and walk in a straight line.
  2. After the first stacks the wind players leave: A one row north, C one row south, and D and B out toward waymarks 1 and 2, just past the middle of the rectangle. That walk is their fourth stack, so they do not move again.
  3. The fire players left on A and C restack onto B or D. Each goes clockwise, A to B and C to D, unless the player there has the same state, both carrying fire or both clear. Then they go counter-clockwise. B and D hold still, so every second stack pairs one fire with one clear player.
  4. Done correctly, every player ends on four Close Caloric stacks, one short of the wipe. Nobody moves again until it fades and Ekpyrosis starts casting.
  5. For Ekpyrosis, go to the walls the exaflares start from, into the gap between the two lanes. Exaflares on the north and south walls: tanks and melee north, healers and ranged south. On the west and east walls: light party 1 west, light party 2 east. Dodge the first exaflare, then spread along the wall in the same order as the Gaiaochos Geocentrism spread: on the north and south walls like its rows (M1, T1, T2, M2 west to east, and R1, H1, H2, R2), on the west and east walls like its columns (M1, T1, H1, R1 north to south, and M2, T2, H2, R2). After the hits, weave back to the middle between the rest.

The wiki describes the same pairings without fixed waymarks. Icy Veins places the Ekpyrosis spreads in the same order as the Geocentrism spread in Gaiaochos, which gives each player an exact position along the wall.

Pangenesis

Pangenesis · Unstable Factor · Pantheos · Factor In · Palladian Grasp

How the mechanic works

Pangenesis deals heavy raidwide damage, split between everyone alive, and hands out debuffs at random, regardless of role:

PlayersDebuffs
TwoNothing
Two1 stack of Unstable Factor
Two2 stacks, plus a 16-second Astral Tilt or Umbral Tilt, one of each
Two2 stacks, plus a 20-second Astral Tilt or Umbral Tilt, one of each
  • Unstable Factor has to be fully gone before it expires, or its holder explodes and wipes the raid. Two players standing together mix: their stacks are added, halved and rounded down for both. A player with no stacks can never gain one, so 2 and 0 leaves 1 and 0, and 1 and 0 leaves both clear. Stable System blocks mixing for the first few seconds.
  • Clearing your last stack gives Critical Factor and leaves a slime behind. Players who never had stacks never get it.
  • Pantheos spawns ten two-player towers in three sets, working inward from the side walls. First one tower per side, one light and one dark. Then two per side, where the north tower is the opposite colour of that side's first tower and the south tower the same. The third set repeats that pattern closer to the middle.
  • Each tower needs one player whose Tilt fits it and one player with no Tilt. Astral Tilt soaks light towers and Umbral Tilt soaks dark towers. Soaking removes the Tilt that was used, then gives one of the two players, at random, the Tilt that matches the tower: a light tower hands out Umbral Tilt and a dark tower Astral Tilt.
  • Afterwards every slime tethers a random player with a tether that can be taken over, and hits its holder with Factor In, a huge AoE that kills anyone carrying Critical Factor. At the same time the main tank is marked for the two-hit Palladian Grasp, which cleaves their half of the room.

How it is solved

The party lines up in a pre-assigned conga line during the cast, with the main tank taking Palladian Grasp at the far east end. That line decides every side, and every tower after that follows from what the player just did.

  1. No-stack players step forward and 1-stack players step back. In each pair the westmost goes west and the eastmost east. No-stack players take the first tower on their side; 1-stack players wait outside, near where the second south tower will spawn. The 2-stack players stay in the line.
  2. When the first towers appear, each 2-stack player goes to the side whose tower their Tilt soaks. The 16-second Tilt soaks that tower with the no-stack player. The 20-second Tilt waits outside and mixes with the 1-stack player, saving its Tilt for the next set.
  3. Second set: the two who soaked the first tower take the north tower on their side, and the two who waited outside take the south one. Both towers are the colour the pair's Tilt now fits.
  4. Third set: if the second tower gave you a Tilt, swap rows; if not, stay in your row. Every third-set tower ends up with exactly one fitting Tilt and one clear player, and every stack is gone by the end.
  5. Stack tightly inside every tower, so each soak is also a mix.
  6. The two no-stack players take the slime tethers, three each: the west one to the north-west corner, the east one north, just east of the middle, staying close enough to step west. The main tank takes Palladian Grasp on the east half with an invulnerability, and everyone else waits south of the middle on the west half.

The conga line order is up to the party. What matters is that it is fixed before the pull and that the main tank sits at the east end, so they are always the eastmost of any pair and stay on the Palladian Grasp side.

The Classical Concepts II

The Classical Concepts · Panta Rhei · Fusion Burst · Palladian Ray · Implode

How the mechanic works

The Classical Concepts returns with massive raid damage and opens exactly like the first time: symbols, Alpha and Beta Target, Shackled Together pairs, and the twelve shapes whose tethers have to be blocked.

  • Panta Rhei is a slow cast that makes every shape vanish and reappear rotated 180 degrees around the middle of the grid, mirrored north to south and west to east at once. The tethers only resolve after it finishes, so players must already be standing on the new positions. The shackles still punish anyone who drifts too far from their partner on the way.
  • Palladian Ray works as before: a tentacle in the west and one in the east, each firing a lingering cone at its four nearest players.
  • Unlike the first time, the shapes Implode after the cones have started going off, so players have to dodge out of the lingering cones into ground that is also clear of every point-blank.

How it is solved

  1. Find your blocking spot exactly as in the first Classical Concepts: your symbol picks the column, your target picks the shape, and the shape no other blue can use settles any ambiguity.
  2. During Panta Rhei, walk to that spot's mirror image through the middle of the grid. The rotated layout has exactly the rotated solution, so every pair doing this blocks every tether. Move slowly with your partner; in Party Finder a jump is a common signal that you are ready.
  3. Icy Veins offers a second method: solve the opposite column's blue from the start and mirror that spot into your own column. It takes more thinking but much less walking across the arena while shackled.
  4. Once the tethers appear, pre-position for Palladian Ray: columns 1 and 2 west, 3 and 4 east, Alpha north and Beta south. Panta Rhei has mirrored every pair across the grid, so the column order is reversed from the first time: west to east green triangle, red circle, purple square, blue ✕.
  5. After your cone hits, step out of it into ground clear of every cone and of every shape's Implode: a gap between four shapes that no cone crosses, or the edge of the floor away from the cones.

Icy Veins notes that the sides and south of the floor are always safe from this final combination with its baiting formation. With the drill's bait spots, the inner columns' cones cross the south middle, so the drill only accepts ground that its cones actually leave clear.

Caloric Theory II

Caloric Theory · Atmosfaction · Close Caloric · Entropifaction · Entropic Excess

How the mechanic works

Caloric Theory returns after Ultima and Crush Helm with the same blue grid and heavy raid damage, but a different set of debuffs. During the cast seven players get a green marker and one gets a red marker. When it ends every player releases a medium AoE around themselves, so the party has to be spread already.

  • Everyone gets Atmosfaction, which works as before: when it expires it knocks back and kills anyone else inside its circle. It resolves at the very end of the mechanic.
  • Green players start on two stacks of Close Caloric and the red player on three. Walking roughly 9 yalms still adds a stack, so anyone who walks more than about 18 yalms, two rectangle diagonals, reaches five and wipes the raid.
  • One random green-marked player, never the red one, gets Entropifaction with eight stacks. Each time a stack falls it drops a large circular AoE, Entropic Excess, on its holder's current spot. It passes to another player on contact, along with its remaining stacks, and the previous holder gets Entropy Resistance, so it can never come back to them. Its holder carries a fiery overhead marker.
  • Ekpyrosis follows, with either of its two patterns, and works as in the first Caloric Theory.

How it is solved

The party pre-assigns a spread on the grid intersections: one player in the middle, six in a hexagon one rectangle diagonal out, and the eighth one diagonal further south-east. Ranged DPS usually take the middle and the extra south-east spot.

  1. Take your spread spot during the cast, exactly on the intersection. If the red marker lands on anyone other than the middle player, those two swap. The red player then sits in the middle for the whole mechanic.
  2. Entropifaction is handed clockwise round the seven outside players, never to the middle. As soon as its AoE appears under you, walk in a straight line to the next outside player and touch them.
  3. When the next AoE appears under the two of you, the new holder walks on to the next player, and the passer steps outward. The usual spot is straight out into the gap between the spot they came from and the one they are standing on, but any spot works that clears this AoE and every later one, stays out of everyone's Atmosfaction, and keeps the passer's whole walk under 18 yalms.
  4. The last outside player has nobody left to pass to. They carry it to the spot the first holder started from, which is empty by then, and stay there for its final drop.
  5. Nobody walks more than two diagonals, so every outside player finishes on four Close Caloric stacks and the middle player stays on three. Everyone is spread when Atmosfaction goes off.

Which slot takes which spread spot is up to the party; the guides only ask for ranged DPS in the middle and the extra spot. The drill seats T1 north, M2 north-east, H2 south-east, R2 far south-east, T2 south, H1 south-west, M1 north-west and R1 in the middle, matching the jobs in Icy Veins' diagram.

Gaiaochos II

Gaiaochos · Summon Darkness · Missing Link · Demi Parhelion · Geocentrism · Divine Excoriation · Ultima Blow

How the mechanic works

The last mechanic before the enrage. Gaiaochos hits the raid, shrinks the floor to a small circle again and gives everyone Ascended, and the first Gaiaochos's pieces return in a new order.

  • Summon Darkness puts a single add on an intercardinal. Its line fires across that whole diagonal, leaving the other two intercardinal corners safe.
  • Demi Parhelion and Geocentrism follow at once, together with Missing Link, which chains every DPS to a support until distance breaks it. This time the tentacles only ever show rows or columns, leaving two narrow lanes clear.
  • As the line and the blasts go off, everyone is hit by Divine Excoriation, a small AoE that punishes anyone standing too close to another player.
  • Summon Darkness is then cast twice more. Each time four adds appear and tether all four players of one role. Each tethered player has Magic Vulnerability Up and is hit by Ultima Blow, a line from their add that has to be shared by one other player. A player caught in two lines dies. Ultima follows each set, and the second set tethers the other role.

How it is solved

  1. Stack in the middle. Note where the add is and which two corners its diagonal leaves safe, then read the tentacles: rows or columns.
  2. When Missing Link appears, supports run to the north safe corner and DPS to the south one, each staying inside the narrow row or column. The two groups end up far apart, which breaks every chain.
  3. Spread along that lane for Divine Excoriation. Healers and ranged stay in the corner, and tanks and melee move further in.
  4. For each Ultima Blow set, the tethered players take their tether straight across to the opposite wall from their add. All four lines then run through the middle and fan apart at the walls.
  5. Each tethered player's partner (as in phase one) stands on that line, just in front of them or out by their add. Too far in towards the middle, the lines crowd together and a blocker takes two. A tank can instead stand in the middle with an invulnerability if someone is dead.

The guides disagree on how many adds each tether set shows: eight at the edge in Icy Veins, four on the Console Games Wiki. The drill shows four, one on each line through the middle, so no two tethers share a line. It pairs partners as T1 with M1, T2 with M2, H1 with R1 and H2 with R2, and orders each spread lane from the corner as H1, H2, T1, T2 and R1, R2, M1, M2, accepting either spot of your job.
